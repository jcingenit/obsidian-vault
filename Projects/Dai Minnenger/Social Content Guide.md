# Social Media Posting Strategy for Dai’s Brand Ecosystem

## Core Strategy

The social media strategy should position Dai as a **music-first creative** whose work expands into wellness, affirmation, and community care.

The goal is not to make DJ services, Sweet Glen’s, and Transcend feel like random separate projects. The goal is to show that all three come from the same larger mission:

> Dai creates community-centered experiences through music, wellness, affirmation, education, and care.

## Primary Social Goal

The main goal should be:

**Get Dai booked for more DJ sets and community-centered events.**

The secondary goals are:

- Grow awareness of Sweet Glen’s
- Build early interest in Transcend
- Strengthen Dai’s identity as a multidisciplinary creative
- Create trust with queer, trans, wellness, music, and local community audiences
- Support small businesses, underrepresented artists, and community partners

---

# Recommended Content Split

Because the website’s main goal is booking sets, the social content should be weighted toward music.

## Suggested Monthly Mix

|Content Category|Percentage|
|---|---|
|DJ Services / Music|50%|
|Community + Personal Brand|20%|
|Sweet Glen’s|15%|
|Transcend|15%|

This keeps Dai’s music identity strong while still introducing the other projects often enough to make the ecosystem feel connected.

# Core Content Pillars

## 1. Music Discovery

**Purpose:** Show Dai’s taste, knowledge, and curatorial value.

Post ideas:

- Underground artist spotlights
- Queer artist recommendations
- “Tracks I’m loving right now”
- Genre education posts
- “Songs that shaped my sound”
- Playlist drops
- Sample breakdowns
- Music history moments
- “What I’m listening to this week”

Example caption angle:

> This track is exactly why I love underground music — it creates a whole world in just a few minutes. More people should know this sound, this artist, and this scene.

CTA:

> Listen, save, share, or follow for more music discovery.

---

## 2. DJ Booking + Event Promotion

**Purpose:** Convert attention into paid sets.

Post ideas:

- Clips from recent sets
- Event flyers
- “Book me for your next event” posts
- Behind-the-scenes setup clips
- Crowd/atmosphere videos
- Testimonials from event organizers
- “What kind of events I DJ” carousel
- “How to book me” post
- Monthly availability post

Example post:

**Slide 1:** Book Dai for Your Next Event  
**Slide 2:** Eclectic DJ sets rooted in underground sound, queer culture, and community  
**Slide 3:** Great for pop-ups, private events, queer-centered nights, creative showcases, and community gatherings  
**Slide 4:** Listen to mixes + send a booking inquiry

CTA:

> Booking inquiries are open. Message me or use the link in bio.

---

## 3. Community + Culture

**Purpose:** Show what Dai stands for beyond the music.

Post ideas:

- Community event recaps
- Local artist/vendor shoutouts
- Queer nightlife appreciation posts
- “Why representation in music matters”
- Support small business posts
- Reflections on creating affirming spaces
- Photos from community events
- “People who inspire the ecosystem” series

Example caption angle:

> A good set is not just about getting people to dance. It is about making the room feel connected, intentional, and alive.

CTA:

> Tag an artist, vendor, or organizer building community right now.

---

## 4. Sweet Glen’s Wellness Content

**Purpose:** Introduce Sweet Glen’s as the wellness side of the ecosystem.

Post ideas:

- Drink spotlights
- Ingredient breakdowns
- “What’s in this juice?” posts
- Personal nutrition reflections
- Small food vendor collaborations
- Wellness tips without medical claims
- Crohn’s-inspired story content
- Market/pop-up announcements
- “Support small food businesses” posts

Example post:

**Slide 1:** What’s in the drink?  
**Slide 2:** Whole-food ingredients  
**Slide 3:** Nutrient-focused choices  
**Slide 4:** Inspired by personal wellness and nutrition research  
**Slide 5:** Try Sweet Glen’s at the next pop-up

CTA:

> Follow Sweet Glen’s for whole-food drinks, nutrition learning, and community wellness.

Important phrasing note: Avoid saying the drinks “treat,” “heal,” or “cure” health conditions. Keep the language focused on nourishment, learning, and healthier choices.

---

## 5. Transcend Affirmation + Resource Content

**Purpose:** Build emotional trust and introduce Transcend as a support-centered project.

Post ideas:

- Affirmation posts
- Planner preview pages
- Resource roundups
- Name change workshop information
- Support tips for allies
- Posts for trans youth and families
- Community organization spotlights
- Reflection prompts
- “What affirmation can look like” series

Example post:

**Slide 1:** Trans people deserve tools made with them in mind.  
**Slide 2:** Transcend is more than a planner.  
**Slide 3:** It is a space for affirmation, reflection, identity, and support.  
**Slide 4:** Resources, prompts, and community care are part of the vision.  
**Slide 5:** Follow along as Transcend grows.

CTA:

> Follow, share with someone who needs this, or connect with us about community resources.

---

## 6. Ecosystem Overlap Content

**Purpose:** Tie all three brands together.

These posts are important because they prevent the audience from seeing the businesses as disconnected.

Post ideas:

- “Music feeds the spirit. Wellness supports the body. Affirmation supports the self.”
- “How Dai’s projects connect”
- “The ecosystem explained”
- “What community care means across my work”
- DJ set + Sweet Glen’s pop-up recap
- Transcend resource table at a music event
- Sweet Glen’s drinks served at a queer-centered event
- “Three ways we build community” carousel

Example carousel:

**Slide 1:** The Dai Ecosystem  
**Slide 2:** Music creates connection  
**Slide 3:** Sweet Glen’s supports wellness  
**Slide 4:** Transcend affirms identity  
**Slide 5:** Community connects it all  
**Slide 6:** Book, listen, support, and follow the work

CTA:

> Start with the music. Stay for the community.

# Content Tone

The tone should feel:

- Intentional
- Community-centered
- Warm
- Knowledgeable
- Creative
- Affirming
- Grounded
- Not overly corporate

Avoid making the brands sound too polished or disconnected from the community. Dai’s strength is the personal, cultural, and values-driven nature of the work.

---

# Visual Direction

## Overall Look

The visuals should feel connected across the ecosystem, but each brand can have its own flavor.

### DJ Services

- Darker, energetic, textured visuals
- Event photos
- Motion blur
- Vinyl, decks, lights, crowds
- Bold typography

### Sweet Glen’s

- Fresh, natural, warm visuals
- Ingredients
- Drinks
- Markets
- Hands, food, preparation
- Earthy tones

### Transcend

- Soft, affirming, clear visuals
- Planner pages
- Quotes
- Community resources
- Calm gradients or clean layouts

## Shared Design Elements

Use consistent:

- Fonts
- Logo placement
- CTA style
- Caption structure
- Color family
- “Dai ecosystem” language

This allows each brand to feel distinct but still connected.
